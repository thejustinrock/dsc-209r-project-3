Healthcare spending and life expectancy

How does the relationship between healthcare spending and life expectancy vary across countries and over time?

Start with 2000 and 2018. The overall relationship strengthened slightly, but spending alone leaves plenty of room for differences between countries. In 2018, Japan spent about $4,504 per person and reached 84.2 years of life expectancy. The U.S. spent about $10,624 and reached 78.6 years.

Spending is current health expenditure per person in purchasing-power-adjusted international dollars (current PPP). The horizontal axis is logarithmic. Life expectancy is measured at birth. Both charts use identical scales. Comparison mode includes only countries with both measures in both selected years. The correlation is Pearson’s r between log spending and life expectancy. It describes an association. Spending changes in current PPP dollars can include price changes.

Source: World Development Indicators, September 2021 snapshot. Regions use World Bank country metadata. World Bank aggregate groups are excluded.
